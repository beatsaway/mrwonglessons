Overview — what counts in VA

Paper 1 (50%) + SBA (50%).

  • 10% Paper 1 Part A — art appreciation · 45 min

    Choose one question. Write about four paragraphs (five if you add a short intro), each with roughly five keyword-rich mark points, using D A I J — Description, Analysis, Interpretation, Judgement.

  • 40% Paper 1 Part B — practical · 3 hr 15 min

    Express the given theme clearly and personally. Establish mid tone quickly, plan perspective and composition, and use symbolic elements that fit the theme literally and figuratively.

  • 30% SBA artworks

    Four pieces (artwork and/or critical studies) showing theme, medium skills, context, creativity, and progression across S5–S6.

  • 20% SBA workbook

    Research workbook covering inspiration, artist references, experimentation, and reflection — all four areas matter for marks.

Part A — appreciation & DAIJ

45 min · 10% · referencing Ms Cho’s notes

The answering framework

D   A   I   J

Description → Analysis → Interpretation → Judgement.

7 Elements of Art C S S F L T V

  • Color: hue, intensity, value; for symbolism, pattern and contrast.
  • Space: positive/negative space, ? point perspective, isometric/orthographic format, foreground/middle/background.
  • Shape: closed, flat forms; geometric or organic.
  • Form: 3D form, real or illusion through perspective/shading.
  • Line: continuous, broken, jagged, vertical, horizontal, diagonal.
  • Texture: real or implied surface quality (rough/smooth/etc.).
  • Value: light-dark range; contrast drives mood and focus.

7 Principles of Art / Design M C U B E P P

  • Movement & Rhythm: eye path and repetition flow.
  • Contrast: differences in size, value, color, etc.
  • Unity & Variety: coherence plus controlled variation.
  • Balance: visual weight (symmetrical, asymmetrical, radial).
  • Emphasis: focal point and attention control.
  • Proportion: scale relationship between parts.
  • Pattern & Repetition: repeated units that build order.

Part B — practical exam tips

Tips from artsyjujube (overall level 5* in DSE VA)

1️⃣ Image bank / visual library

A binder or folder of reference images for the exam — photos and other artists’ work (to see how drawing differs from photos).

Collect by category (2–4 images each): animals, plants, people, festive decorations, famous faces, everyday objects, etc.

Why it helps: if you forget how to draw something, flip to that section — saves time and panic. Also collect composition examples (foreground / midground / background) to plan layout fast.

2️⃣ Time management during the exam

  • Sketch / draft: 10–15 min — composition and layout only, not detail
  • Colouring: remaining time minus the final hour
  • Final detailing: last 1–1.5 hr — highlights, linework on a fully coloured piece
  • Last 15 min: artist’s statement, sign name, attach any extra sketches

⚠️ Do not skip the written reflection — markers read it. Explain materials, story, and what you drew.

3️⃣ How to practise before the exam

Past papers: 6–7 years’ worth (often done in class with teacher).

At home: less often full 4-hour papers; more often repeat the same reference to master colour, brushwork, and detail on familiar subjects — so you do not waste thinking time in the exam.

4️⃣ Techniques for Level 5+

  • Repeat the same subject until you can draw it fast and well (people, animals, etc.).
  • Develop a consistent, readable style — exaggerated drama and expression often reads well to markers.
  • Creative storytelling: most candidates pick the same obvious story; ask who, when, where, why for a unique angle. Show the story in the drawing and state it in the reflection. A bad story is worse than a simple safe one.

5️⃣ Finishing the drawing on time 👍👍👍👍👍

Running out of time is the biggest disaster. Use strict stage timers (e.g. 10 min sketch — then stop). Example split: 1 hr main figure, 30 min background, 1 hr highlights. Master this before DSE. ⏰ Speed-run practice helps.

6️⃣ Part A (appreciation) — past papers & writing speed

  • Past papers help, but last-minute cramming has limited value.
  • Better: read high-scoring HKEAA examples and focus on technique.
  • Writing speed is critical — write continuously from the moment you see the question.

*This YouTuber scored ~15–16 / ~20 on the English appreciation paper (normally 18–20 at school).

SBA — artworks & workbook

School-based assessment (50%) · artsyjujube SBA tips (96/100)

Four artwork / critical-study pieces (30%) plus research workbook (20%). Work spans S5 and S6; at least one piece should be practical artwork.

Artworks (30%) — what markers look for

Workbook (20%) — four assessment areas

AreaWhat to show
Inspiration & researchWhere ideas came from; research and references that shaped your concept
Artist referencesArtists studied; skills learned; how you applied them (+ small copies)
ExperimentationTrying media, techniques, compositions — compare and choose
Reflection & evaluationProblems found, changes made, before/after evidence, what you learned

Teachers mark whether your portfolio covers all four areas evenly.

Workbook pages — do & don’t

❌ Avoid✅ Prefer
Large pasted images just to fill spaceSmall, meaningful images to the side (OK if you write over them)
Random decorative drawingEvery mark shows learning or decision-making
Long copied textbook passagesYour own thoughts and how ideas apply to your work
Neglecting one of the four areasDevelop all four throughout the portfolio

Each page can mix writing, drawing, and pasted items — a page with no drawing is fine if the content is purposeful.

Area 1 — Inspiration & research

  • Explain how initial inspiration guided your direction and final idea.
  • Example (theme: elderly people): why a clock? what from a documentary or article? paste images and respond in writing.
  • Use personal experience linked to the theme; note quotes from talks/videos and how they shaped imagery or materials.

Area 2 — Artist references

  • Brief style intro — do not copy long biographies.
  • Focus on skills/techniques learned and how you applied them in your work.
  • Include a small copy or detail study (e.g. three pen artists if your final piece is pen).

Area 3 — Experimentation

TypeExample
MediaPen on white vs white pen on black paper
TechniqueThree ways to render tree bark
CompositionVertical vs horizontal vs diagonal layout

Show comparisons, note pros/cons, state what you chose and why. Layout experiments count too.

Area 4 — Reflection & evaluation

  • Photograph work-in-progress before fixing mistakes.
  • Before/after comparisons; what went wrong, what you changed, what you learned.
  • Short, focused reflections — not a long essay.

Workbook rubric (20%)

AreaStrong performance
Exploration and development of theme or ideasDiverse sources; transforms and integrates material into original ideas
Interpretation of artworks & link to artmaking / critical studiesMultiple contexts and formal qualities; informed personal views with evidence
Experimentation of media and skillsAbundant, thorough experiments and exploration of expression
Reflection and progressionContinuous reflection from diverse angles; substantial progression

Final SBA tips

  • Quality over quantity — excellent pages beat many filler pages.
  • Take progress photos for reflection sections.
  • Describe ideas clearly; include analysis and reflection throughout.

Exam rules & materials

HKEAA Instructions for Individual Subjects: HKDSE VA (PDF)

Key rules

◆ Part A: answers in the answer book; collected 45 minutes after the exam starts (~10 min per paragraph).

◆ Part B: draw only on HKEAA white cartridge paper or Xuanzhi — other paper normally means zero for that part.

◆ Do not write your name or ID on cartridge or bond paper.

◆ Do not cover cartridge paper with extra A2-size sheets (Paper 1 only; not Paper 2).

◆ Do not cut cartridge paper — penalty if paper is cut or not the paper provided.

◆ “Materials NOT Allowed” → mark penalty; dangerous materials / smoking devices → disqualification.

◆ Reference materials are for reference only; invigilators may inspect what you bring. No cutting boards at centres.

Materials NOT allowed

English and Chinese dictionaries (including excerpts), slow-drying materials (e.g. oil paint), retarder, dangerous materials (aerosol paints, air-brushes, aerosol fixatives, etc.), burning and smoking devices.

Materials allowed 👍

Paper 1: painting tools (pencils, brushes, colours, palettes, boards, clips, pins, erasers, tape, paper, etc.), small collage materials, reference books/magazines/dictionaries.

Paper 2: design/drawing tools (rulers, compasses, colour/adhesive paper, transfer letters, stencils, etc.), cutting/sticking/collage materials, reference books/specimens/magazines/scrapbooks/dictionaries.
