Part A — DAIJ & keywords Page 1

DAIJ sentence frames

Description

  • The commonalities of plate (a) and plate (b) is that both are ______.
  • In plate (a), I can see ______.
  • In plate (b), there is / there are ______.
  • Both artworks present ______ in different ways.
  • A key visual feature in both works is ______.

Analysis

  • In plate (a), the artist uses ______ to show ______.
  • In plate (b), the artist uses ______, creating a ______ feeling.
  • The contrast between ______ and ______ strengthens ______.
  • This arrangement creates a sense of ______ through ______.

Interpretation

  • In plate (a), I think the artist wants to show ______.
  • In plate (b), I think the artist wants to show ______.
  • _____ may symbolize ______ in the social/cultural context.
  • The ______ mood suggests ideas of ______ and ______, hinted by ______.

Judgement

  • I think plate (a) is a success because ______.
  • I think plate (b) is / is not successful because ______.
  • I like plate (__) more because ______.
  • Overall, the stronger work is plate (__) because ______.
  • The most convincing visual decision is ______.

C S S F L T V — 7 Elements

Color
Hue, intensity, value; symbolism, pattern, contrast.
Space
Positive/negative space, perspective, foreground/middle/background.
Shape
Closed flat forms; geometric or organic.
Form
3D form — real or illusion through perspective/shading.
Line
Continuous, broken, jagged; vertical, horizontal, diagonal.
Texture
Real or implied surface quality.
Value
Light–dark range; contrast drives mood and focus.

M C U B E P P — 7 Principles

Movement & Rhythm
Eye path and repetition flow.
Contrast
Differences in size, value, colour, etc.
Unity & Variety
Coherence plus controlled variation.
Balance
Visual weight (symmetrical, asymmetrical, radial).
Emphasis
Focal point and attention control.
Proportion
Scale relationship between parts.
Pattern & Repetition
Repeated units that build order.

Part B — exam tips Page 2

From artsyjujube (overall level 5*) · Paper 1: Part A 45 min + Part B 3 hr 15 min

1 Image bank / visual library

A binder of reference images for the exam — photos and other people’s artworks (to show photo vs drawing).

Collect by category (2–4 images each): animals, plants, people, CNY decorations, famous people, everyday objects, compositions from other artists (layout, foreground/mid/background).

Why: flip to a section instantly if you forget how to draw something; saves time and panic.

2 Time management

  • Sketch: 10–15 min — composition and basic layout only.
  • Colour: all remaining time except the last hour.
  • Detail: last 1–1.5 hr — highlights, linework on a fully coloured piece.
  • Last 15 min: artist statement, sign name, add sketches/drafts.

Don’t skip the written reflection — markers read it. Explain materials, story, and what you drew.

3 How to practise

  • Past papers: 6–7 years at school (teacher-led).
  • At home: repeat specific skills — same reference many times for colour, brushstrokes, details.
  • Goal: know certain subjects so well you don’t waste time thinking in the exam.

4 Level 5+ techniques

  • Repetition: draw the same subject (e.g. old lady) the same way each time — faster and better.
  • Consistent style: exaggerated, dramatic stories and expressions often score well.
  • Creative storytelling: most candidates pick the same story — ask who, when, where, why. Write it in your reflection and show it in the drawing. A bad story is worse than a simple safe one.

5 Finish the drawing

Extremely important — running out of time is the biggest disaster.

  • Set a timer per stage; stop when time is up and move on.
  • Example split: 1 hr main character, 30 min background, 1 hr highlights.
  • Master this before DSE. Try speed-run techniques.

6 Theory / appreciation prep

  • Past papers help, but last-minute cramming won’t do much.
  • Study high-scoring answers (HKEAA level 5 examples) and technique.
  • Writing speed is critical — write non-stop from the moment you see the question.

  • Quality over quantity — excellent pages beat many average ones.
  • Every page needs a purpose; no filler.
  • Take progress photos for reflection.
  • Describe ideas in effective or poetic terms; include reflections and analysis.

SBA — workbook detail 20% Page 4

Teachers mark whether your portfolio covers all four areas.

AreaWhat to include
#1 Inspiration & research How initial inspiration guided direction; link inspiration to final idea. Use personal experience, documentaries, pasted images with your thoughts. Note key quotes from videos/talks.
#2 Artist references Artist style and techniques (brief facts only). Focus on skills learned and how you applied them. Include a small copy or detail study — e.g. three pen artists if your final piece is pen.
#3 Experimentation Side-by-side comparisons of media, techniques, compositions. Observations, pros/cons, which you chose and why. Try layout experiments, not just technique.
#4 Reflection WIP photos before erasing or overpainting; before/after comparisons. What problem, how you improved, what changed. Challenges and key learning points — short, not a long story.

HKEAA exam rules Page 5

Candidate Handbook — HKDSE VA (PDF)

  • Part A: write in the answer book; collected 45 minutes after the exam starts.
  • Part B: draw on HKEAA white cartridge paper or xuan paper only — unauthorised paper → script not accepted (normally zero).
  • Do not write your name or ID on cartridge or bond paper.
  • Do not cover cartridge paper with ~A2-size sheets (Paper 1 only; not Paper 2).
  • Do not cut the cartridge paper — penalty if paper is cut or not the one provided.
  • “Materials NOT Allowed” → mark penalty; dangerous materials / smoking devices → disqualification.
  • Reference materials are for reference only; invigilators may inspect what you bring. No cutting boards at centres.

Materials NOT allowed

English and Chinese dictionaries (including excerpts), slow-drying materials (e.g. oil paint), retarder, dangerous materials (aerosol paints, air-brushes, aerosol fixatives, etc.), burning and smoking devices.

Materials allowed

Paper 1: pencils, brushes, colours, palettes, boards, clips, pins, erasers, tape, paper; small collage materials; reference books, magazines, art dictionaries.

Paper 2: drawing instruments and aids, rulers, compasses, colour/adhesive paper, transfer letters, stencils, cutting/sticking/collage materials, reference books, type specimens, magazines, scrapbooks, art dictionaries.
